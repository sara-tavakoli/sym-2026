# VisitReady: Problem Evidence

Research pack for the "Problem Evidence & Pitch Confidence" criterion. Every figure below was checked against the source listed (abstract, full text or official page) on 18 Oct 2026, unless it is marked **[secondary]** or **[unverified]**. Citation numbers refer to section 6.

Strength key: **PR** = peer-reviewed · **GOV** = government / statutory body · **PROF** = professional body (RACGP) · **IND** = industry / company claim · **NEWS** = media.

---

## 1. Headline stats for the pitch

1. **40–80%** of medical information from clinicians is forgotten immediately, and almost half of what is remembered is wrong. [1] (PR review)
2. **4 of 35** GP patients raised everything they came in to discuss. Every problem outcome in the study was linked to something left unsaid. [3] (PR, BMJ)
3. **11 seconds**: median time before a clinician interrupted a patient's opening concerns, when the clinician asked for them at all (asked in only 36% of visits). [5] (PR)
4. **Up to 62%** of lab tests and **up to 35.7%** of imaging tests were not followed up in ambulatory care (range across 19 studies). [6] (PR, Australian authors)
5. **3.0 million** Australians are unpaid carers (11.9% of people in households). [8] (GOV, ABS 2022) In a national carer survey, **81.9%** helped with health care. [9] (PR abstract)
6. **59%** of Australians aged 15–74 scored below the level needed for adequate health literacy (ABS, 2006, still the most recent figure on that measure). [10] (GOV)

Optional context line: Australian GPs report an average consult of **19.7 minutes** in 2025 [13], with **154 problems managed per 100 encounters** (BEACH 2015–16) [14]. Many visits cover more than one problem.

## 2. Evidence by problem

### BEFORE: patients arrive unprepared and leave things unsaid

| Claim | Figure | Strength | Ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patients in GP consultations who voiced **all** their agenda items | 4 of 35. "No one had only one agenda item and most had five or more." | PR | [3] |
| Items most often left unsaid | Worries about diagnosis and the future, the patient's own ideas about the illness, side effects, not wanting a prescription, social context | PR | [3] |
| Consequences of unvoiced items | In all 14 consultations with "problem outcomes" (major misunderstandings, unwanted or unused prescriptions, non-adherence), at least one problem was linked to an unvoiced item | PR | [3] |
| Australians who find it difficult to discuss health concerns with providers | 12%. Only 33% find it always easy; 56% usually | GOV | [11] |
| Australians who always find it easy to navigate the health system | 26% (17% among those with very high psychological distress) | GOV | [11] |
| Health literacy below adequate (ages 15–74) | ~59% ("below Level 3 for the health domain") | GOV (2006) | [10] |
| **Does preparation help?** Pre-consultation interventions (question prompt lists, coaching) | Small but significant increase in question asking (SMD 0.27, 95% CI 0.19–0.36) and satisfaction (SMD 0.09). **No significant increase in consultation length** (0.10, −0.05 to 0.25) | PR (systematic review of RCTs) | [16] |
| Question prompt lists | 42 studies / 50 interventions. A physician-endorsed QPL given just before the consult may increase question asking; effects on recall, anxiety and satisfaction were inconsistent | PR review (Australian authors, work commissioned by ACSQHC) | [17] |

**Pitch use:** patients bring several concerns and rarely voice them all. Preparing beforehand is evidence-based and *does not lengthen the consult*, which answers the "GPs don't have time" objection. Be honest that the effect size is small. [16]

**Pitch use:** the consult is short and covers many problems, and patients forget much of it. A written plan they can take away is one of the few recall aids with consistent support [18]. People share recordings with family (68%) [19], which supports the Care Circle feature.

## 3. What HotDoc / Heidi / OnEarly do today (public facts only)

**HotDoc** (Australian GP booking platform)
- Practice site lists: Online Bookings, Reminders, Forms, Telehealth, Mobile Check-in, Recalls (clinical reminders). [21]
- Claims "23,000+" practitioners, "over 14 million patients" helped, and "1 in 3" Australians use HotDoc. [21] (IND)
- Feb 2026: Potentia Capital took a majority stake; media reported a ~A$250–300m valuation (not confirmed by the parties); 13M patients cited. [22] (NEWS)
- No public current count of practices found. A 2021 trade estimate said "about half of all GP practices" (old and not verified, so don't quote).
- **Gap:** nothing public on patient-side preparation (question prioritising, symptom timeline), patient-held after-visit plans, follow-up task tracking, or family/carer delegation.

**Heidi Health** (Australian AI scribe)
- An ambient scribe that "Listens", "Writes" notes and documents, "Answers" clinical questions, and "Acts" on admin. Claims "175M+ patient interactions supported worldwide". [23] (IND)
- **Already has a "Patient recap"**: "A plain-language summary of what you discussed, the plan and any medication changes", and "Nothing goes to the patient until you've read the recap and approved it." [23] **We must not claim after-visit summaries are new.** What is new: the summary becomes a *tracked checklist the patient and carer own*, linked to the patient's pre-visit brief and to HotDoc booking for follow-ups.
- AI scribe adoption by Australian GPs: newsGP poll, 22% (Aug 2024, n=1,170) → 40% (Nov 2025, n=1,516). Self-selected poll. [20] (PROF/NEWS) A Healthed/Medical Republic survey of 1,535 GPs found 18.7% *personally* use one; the wording differed. Avant: 11% → 19% of members (Aug 2024 → Feb 2025). [20]

**OnEarly** (onearly.com)
- Care coordination platform: "Your care. Finally in one place." Centralises forms, records and conversations; tasks with clear ownership; progress over time; family sharing, with the individual keeping control of their information. Mentions PathPlan (NDIS access) and OneMRI. [24] (IND)
- The site does not state its location; the NDIS references suggest Australia. It gives no user numbers.
- **Gap VisitReady fills:** OnEarly is general care coordination. VisitReady ties the care circle to a *specific GP appointment* (brief → plan → follow-up tasks) inside the booking flow patients already use.

**Also relevant (possible judge question):** Healthdirect (Australian Government) hosts a "Questions to ask your doctor" page (healthdirect.gov.au/question-builder). We could only confirm the page title, not its features. VisitReady differs by building the questions from the patient's own symptom timeline and sending them to the clinician.

## 4. Likely judge questions + honest answers

**Q1. Is this a real problem, or just a nice-to-have?**
A: Three independent lines of evidence. Patients forget 40–80% of what they're told [1]. Only 4 in 35 raise everything they came for, and unsaid items were behind every bad outcome in that study [3]. Up to 62% of lab results go without follow-up in some ambulatory settings [6]. These are peer-reviewed findings, not anecdotes.

**Q2. Won't this make GP consults longer?**
A: A BMJ systematic review of RCTs of pre-consult preparation found no significant change in consultation length, along with a small increase in question asking and satisfaction [16]. We also keep the brief to one page so it can be read in under a minute.

**Q3. Heidi already sends patient summaries. What's new?**
A: Yes, Heidi's Patient recap exists and inspired us [23]. Our addition is the loop around it: a patient-made brief going *in*, the plan coming *back* as a checklist with owners (patient or carer), and follow-up bookings made through HotDoc. Heidi serves the clinician; VisitReady serves the patient and family.

**Q4. Isn't the 40–80% figure old?**
A: It comes from a 2003 review of older studies [1], and "almost half remembered incorrectly" comes from one 1979 rheumatology clinic study [2]. We present it as "the range reported in the literature". A 2009 systematic review supports the remedy: written instructions improve recall [18].

**Q5. Do you have Australian evidence?**
A: Yes: ABS (3.0M carers [8], 59% low health literacy [10], 12% find it hard to discuss concerns with providers [11]), RACGP (19.7-min consults [13]), BEACH (154 problems per 100 encounters [14]), and the follow-up review by Australian researchers [6]. The agenda and interruption studies are UK/US [3][4][5].

**Q6. Is this a medical device? Is it safe?**
A: It's a hackathon concept, not a medical device, and has had no clinical validation. It gives no diagnosis or advice. It reorganises the patient's own words, traced back to their input, and keeps medication text verbatim from the clinician because misreading instructions is common [15]. Any production version would need a TGA software-as-a-medical-device assessment, Privacy Act / APP compliance, and clinical safety review.

**Q7. What data is in the demo?**
A: Synthetic patients and synthetic plans only. No real patient data. AI outputs are drafts the patient reviews, and everything in the brief links back to the patient's own words.

**Q8. Will GPs actually read it?**
A: Not proven yet. Evidence suggests clinician endorsement matters: QPLs worked best when physician-endorsed and given just before the consult [17]. The rapid rise in AI scribe use (22% → 40% in a newsGP poll [20]) shows GPs will adopt tools that save time. Our answer is to keep it to one page, put the patient's priorities first, and fit it into HotDoc check-in. We'd validate this with GP interviews next.

## 5. Suggested 30-second user interviews (run with 3–5 people today)

Intro: "We're building a student concept to help people get more from GP visits. 30 seconds, five quick questions, nothing recorded with your name."

1. "Think of your last GP visit. Did you leave with a question you didn't get to ask?" (Yes / No / Can't remember)
2. "After the visit, did you forget or get confused about anything the doctor said: a test, referral or medication?" (Yes / No; if yes, what?)
3. "Have you ever not followed up on a referral, test or follow-up booking you were told to make? Why?"
4. "Does anyone else (partner, parent, child) help you with appointments, or do you help someone else?" (Who / what tasks)
5. "If you could get a one-page 'what to do next' checklist after the visit, shared with someone you choose, would you use it? 1–5."

Capture: tally yes/no answers, write down one verbatim quote per person, and record age band and carer status. Report it as "n=X informal conversations, not a study". Don't overclaim.

### Misattribution / caution notes for Q&A
- **"40–80% forgotten"** is Kessels' summary of earlier studies, not a new measurement. The sentence carries no direct citation in [1].
- **"Half of what's remembered is incorrect"** traces to a single small 1979 rheumatology clinic study [2], not to GP patients generally.
- **"Doctors interrupt after 11 / 18 / 23 seconds"** comes from three different US studies with different measures (median vs mean, with or without the agenda being asked) [4][5]. Say "within about 11–23 seconds" and name the study. A published letter argued the 11-second median may overstate the problem, because uninterrupted patients took a median of only 6 s.
- **"60% of Australians have low health literacy"** is the 2006 ABS figure (59%) on a literacy-skills scale [10]. The 2018 ABS survey used a different, self-report instrument and is far more positive (e.g. 97% agree they have enough information to manage their health) [11]. Always say "2006".
- **RACGP consult length** is GP self-report, not measured [12][13].
- **HotDoc patient numbers** vary by source (8M, 13M, 14M). Use HotDoc's own "over 14 million patients" with attribution [21].
